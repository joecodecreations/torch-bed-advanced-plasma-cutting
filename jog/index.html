How far the torch can travel, in mm. Units work too (24in, 60cm). Type it, or teach it with the corner buttons below.

Teach the corners

Front-left is the zero corner of the grid. Jog the torch there and press its button. Then jog to the back-right corner, diagonally opposite, and press that one: it sets width and depth in one go. If an arrow turns out to be back to front, it is corrected for you. If the picture is the wrong way round for how you stand at the table, press Turn the picture until zero sits in the corner you expect; these buttons and the grid follow it.

Free travel ignores the bed limits, for when the position on screen no longer matches the machine. It switches off when you set zero.

Torch relay

Wire the relay's signal input to Arduino pin A3 (labelled CoolEn on a CNC shield) and its ground to GND. Use a relay board that switches on with a HIGH signal.

Direction setup

The arrows should move the torch the same way on the table as on the screen: → to the right, and ↑ ↓ up and down the grid. Tick a box to turn just that pair of arrow keys round; it does not turn the picture or change where Move torch here goes. Teaching the corners sets which way the machine counts.

The app

Stops and starts TorchBed itself, which also restarts the controller. Use it after an update, or if something is stuck. Your drawing and settings are kept, but zero has to be set again afterwards.

Firmware

Flashing first saves a copy of what is on the board into firmware/backups, then writes and checks the new firmware. It takes about 20 seconds.

Flash new firmware?

A backup of the current firmware is saved first. Keep the USB cable plugged in until it finishes. The torch must be off and the machine stopped. Your $ settings stay on the board.

To calibrate: engrave the test square on scrap of this metal for a set number of passes, measure how deep it went, and type both numbers above. Depth per pass drops as a pocket gets deeper, so repeat at the depth you expect to work at.

Maps are saved as images: white means leave alone, darker means go deeper. Laser software such as LightBurn and EZCAD can engrave to depth from them, and they work as a guide for milling or grinding.
